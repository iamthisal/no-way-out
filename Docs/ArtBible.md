## Shared style

Public rooms share plaster, muted marble/wood floors, dark timber, stone, dull brass and glass cases. Reuse the same frames, cases, pedestals and trim. Storage and staff areas use concrete, metal shelving, crates and industrial fixtures. Keep damage restrained: tilted frames, dusty surfaces and a few displaced props rather than debris across every passage.

## Five-gallery extension

| Gallery | Simple thematic cues |
|---|---|
| Ancient Egypt | One replica-style artifact or labelled display, warm stone accents |
| Stone Age | Small tool/stone displays with simple informative labels |
| Greece / Rome | Sculpture or pottery displays using the same plinth kit |
| Asian Heritage | Clearly labelled, specific cultural artifacts; avoid presenting unrelated cultures as interchangeable |
| Natural History | Fossil, mineral or specimen displays; avoid requiring a large animated animal |

Each new room is 7 × 8 m between wall centres. Keep theme differences in displays and labels, not incompatible architecture or shaders. Use licensed assets where appropriate and record sources. Student 3 still creates the required original models. Do not invent factual artifact labels without research when creating final exhibit content.

## Lighting and movement

Use the manual's provisional lights, then evaluate with ceilings active. All five galleries share the public material palette and warm lighting family. Darker mood must still allow players to read enemies and openings. The central open-air gap stays inaccessible behind the locked glass door D19, with visual-only ground and no courtyard NavMesh; no playable courtyard is required.

D13–D18 are 2.8 m wide × 2.5 m high. Preserve their approach space and primary routes when replacing greybox props. Re-test player and enemy movement after art/collider changes. Student 1 handles materials, lighting and static props; working doors and movable objects belong to Student 2.
